Research Question:  How can transparent tip distribution and private concern reporting improve employee trust and address workplace power imbalances? 

 

# Part 1: The People 

Internal stakeholder, Aoife Rennick (CEO): Aoife states, "Make it work on a phone" (messages/04-aoife.md). She needs a commercially viable, mobile-friendly product that appeals to restaurant owners while remaining accessible to employees. 

External stakeholder, Rafael (Kitchen Porter): Rafael states, "I don't understand how they count it" and "please don't say it was me who ask" (messages/01-rafael.md). His need for transparent payout calculations and private concern reporting reflects the power imbalance between employers and employees. 

Cultural difference and accessibility: Rafael's first language is Portuguese, and his phone screen is damaged. The application will therefore provide Portuguese language support, simple explanations, large text, high contrast and clear navigation. 


# Part 4a: Theory Behind the Build 

Leadership Theory: Servant Leadership 

Greenleaf's (1970) servant leadership theory prioritises serving and empowering others. Declan's statement, "They don't need the formula, they need the money" (messages/02-declan.md), contrasts with this approach by limiting employees' understanding of their earnings. My design reflects servant leadership through transparent payout calculations, accessible language options and the Tip Time Machine, giving employees greater understanding of decisions affecting them. However, the theory does not fully address Sciar's commercial dependence on restaurant owners. 

 

Organisational Culture: Psychological Safety 

Edmondson (1999) defines psychological safety as a shared belief that interpersonal risk-taking is safe. Niamh claims, "Nobody has ever complained about the split" (messages/03-niamh.md), while Rafael states, "please don't say it was me who ask" (messages/01-rafael.md). This suggests that employee silence may reflect fear rather than satisfaction. My design addresses this through private concern reporting and the Concern Evidence Builder. 

 

Competitive Advantage and Limitations 

An employee-centred approach could differentiate Sciar through transparency, accessibility and employee empowerment, potentially strengthening trust and customer loyalty. However, restaurant owners may resist increased transparency, creating a barrier to adoption. Psychological safety also cannot be established through technology alone, particularly when concern reporting is simulated. Genuine improvement requires management to listen and respond without retaliation. 
 

# Part 4b: Decision Record 

Decision 1: Stakeholder Request Refused 

Declan requested that the points table remain hidden from employees (messages/02-declan.md). I considered hiding the formula, providing limited information or displaying it transparently. My criteria were employee understanding, privacy and workplace relationships. Rafael's difficulty understanding his earnings influenced my decision to display the formula while protecting colleagues' individual earnings. This addresses Declan's concern by limiting personal comparisons between employees. Evidence of privacy risks would have changed the level of information displayed. 

 
Decision 2: AI Suggestion Rejected 

The AI-generated market scan recommended claiming that restaurants retain staff 34% longer and that Sciar was the only fully compliant platform (messages/05-market-scan.md). I considered accepting, modifying or rejecting these claims based on accuracy, verifiability and customer trust. I rejected both because supporting evidence was unavailable, replacing them with facts from the case pack. Reliable research could have changed my decision regarding retention, while legal claims remain excluded under the assessment requirements. 

 
Personal Bias 

I noticed solution bias during the development of the interactive features, when I became more focused on innovation and appearance than Rafael's actual needs. I recognised that advanced technology does not necessarily improve user experience. Instead, I prioritised accessibility, clear payout explanations and private concern reporting. 

 

# Part 4c: The Struggle 

While developing an interactive application that deals with employee needs and  business interests, the restaurant photographs on the landing page failed to load, despite having multiple attempts to fix them. I decided to remove external images as a whole and did a different design using product designs. I noticed that the AI-generated landing page had images that weren't loading and decided an alternative design. I tested the staff application and made sure that its functionality worked. This experience taught me that independent testing and human-centred design is also important when it comes to AI-generated code and if I tested the features earlier, I could of avoided unnecessary external reliance before adding visual improvements.
